Why this one

It looks like something a lab already owns. A stock book on a shelf next to the reagents — ruled columns, typewriter numerals, rubber stamps for state. Nothing on screen looks like software.

  • Stamps do the work sideways, so scanning a column tells you what's low
  • Numbers in a monospace column actually line up — you can compare at a glance
  • Margin notes are written in the gutter, like a real ledger

Best if you like the current paper feel and just want it more grown-up and legible.

Why this one

Built for reading across a bench. Big tabular numbers you can read from a metre away, a coloured edge showing state without reading anything, and one accent colour so attention goes where the problem is.

  • Nobody has to squint to find "18 g" or "0 L"
  • Dark by default so a bright screen doesn't wash out the bench
  • Use and restock live on the card — two taps to log anything

Best if speed matters more than charm, and you mostly use it standing up with gloves on.
